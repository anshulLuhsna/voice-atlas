A reader says “I emailed Dr. Sen on 3/4.” To produce audio it must decide how to expand the title, interpret the date convention, pronounce the name, group the phrase and generate a pressure waveform. A beautiful voice can fluently read the wrong date. Keep the decisions separately inspectable wherever the architecture allows.

## First choose the sounds

Normalize ambiguous text with surrounding context and locale. Expanding 3/4 to a calendar date, a fraction or the literal symbols gives different target sounds. Map the chosen wording to phones using a lexicon or grapheme-to-phoneme model. Some pipelines learn directly from characters or bytes, leaving pronunciation decisions implicit. Keep the original span and pronunciation override so a correction to *Sen* survives a later voice change. Segment on phrase boundaries while retaining paragraph context for emphasis and abbreviation resolution.

A concatenative system chooses recorded units that match phonetic context and prosody. Its search balances a mismatch cost for each candidate against an acoustic discontinuity cost at each join. Two individually excellent units can sound spliced if pitch or spectral envelope jumps at the seam. A library recorded from one speaker can be convincing within its coverage, yet an unseen style or context may have poor candidates. [Unit-selection research](#source/sp-join)

Statistical parametric synthesis predicts trajectories such as spectral envelope, F0 and duration from linguistic context, then uses a vocoder to turn parameters into a signal. A small representation permits systematic control and yields smooth transitions. Maximizing likelihood under an averaging model can excessively smooth distinct realizations. Toda and Tokuda investigated a variance term to recover variation in generated trajectories. Use this as a diagnostic: if the predicted features already lack variation, changing only the final speaker or playback codec offers little leverage. [Parameter generation and global variance](#source/sp-parametric)

## Predict acoustics, then predict samples

In Tacotron 2 an acoustic network maps text to mel spectrogram frames and a conditional WaveNet vocoder generates waveform samples from them. Text-to-frame alignment must account for the different durations of phones. Attention can skip text or loop on a phrase. A duration-based architecture predicts how many frames each phone occupies; the expanded sequence allows frame prediction in parallel. FastSpeech 2 also conditions on pitch and energy. With a 12.5 ms hop, durations of 4, 8 and 6 frames span 50, 100 and 75 ms respectively. Changing the middle duration from 8 to 12 adds 50 ms to the phrase and moves every following audio-to-text boundary. Resynthesize the alignment when changing delivery. [Tacotron 2](#source/tacotron) · [FastSpeech 2](#source/sp-fastspeech)

A waveform at 24 kHz has 24,000 samples per second. Autoregressive sample generation conditions each new sample on previous ones, creating a serial dependency. A diffusion vocoder can start from noise and refine a block through a sequence of denoising steps conditioned on acoustic features. Parallel work within a step trades against the number of steps and required context. A conditional flow-matching model learns velocities along a path from noise to desired speech and integrates those velocities at synthesis. Voicebox uses text and surrounding audio for speech infilling. Step counts, chunk boundaries, conditioning and hardware all influence first-audible latency. [DiffWave](#source/sp-diffwave) · [Voicebox](#source/voicebox)

## Separate speaker, style and text

Speaker identity reflects relatively stable voice cues across phrases. Style and emotion involve choices about rate, prominence, breath, phrasing and pitch movement in a particular passage. In one cloning architecture, a speaker encoder produces an embedding from a reference recording, an acoustic model conditions on that embedding, and a vocoder renders the acoustics. A short noisy reference can also encode room or prosodic cues. Enrollment requires the speaker's consent and scope of use. A more similar timbre may coexist with worse pronunciation or inconsistent character intent. [Speaker-conditioned synthesis](#source/sp-cloning)

Across a book, text segmentation changes the available context. A pronoun at the start of a new chunk may refer to a different speaker than a local model assumes. Maintain a pronunciation lexicon, character-to-voice mapping, rate range and scene-direction record keyed to the document revision. Read adjacent passages together before changing voice. A sentence may contain a quotation followed by narrator attribution, requiring more than one segment. Ambiguous attribution should have a conservative default that preserves the text.

## Exercise · localize an error

Take the line “I said **Thursday**, not Tuesday.” Write three intended readings: correcting the day, correcting who spoke, and a plain report. Mark the focus word and a proposed pause in each. Compare a fixed narrator with an expressive plan while holding wording and voice constant. Then hold the plan constant and change only the voice. Ask a blinded listener which correction they heard and whether any word changed. Expect intended emphasis to track the delivery plan more directly than a change of speaker. Disagreement identifies where the script, model controls or acoustic rendering need a closer look.

## Check your reasoning

**A vocoder yields clean audio, but “Dr.” becomes “Drive” before a name. Where should the first trace begin?** Inspect the text normalization and pronunciation sequence. A clean waveform can accurately render the wrong expansion.

**Would pitch-shifting an entire paragraph guarantee a new character?** A uniform shift changes one acoustic cue. Character identity and intention can also depend on articulation, relative emphasis, timing and the narrative attribution. Test listener recognition across unfamiliar lines, including quiet passages.
