A research gap is a discrepancy between a desired outcome and what a specified system can establish. It has a population, a comparison and a consequence of being wrong. The four proposals here are hypotheses. The sources support particular mechanisms or observations; each proposed benefit needs its own test.

## 1 · Can a listener reliably resume at the last understood phrase?

**Who and why.** Consider someone listening to a source article who interrupts to ask about the last sentence. Three boundaries may diverge: generated audio, device presentation and the words the person understood. EPUB media overlays already associate text fragments with timed audio. ElevenReader advertises bookmarks. Twilio offers completion marks for sent audio and also returns pending marks when the buffer is cleared. That last detail gives an implementation mechanism and a counterexample to treating every returned mark as audible completion. These sources establish tools and protocol behavior. They do not measure how often readers lose their place. [EPUB media overlays](#source/rt-epub) · [Twilio stream messages](#source/rt-twilio) · [Reader feature listing](#source/reader-price)

**Hypothesis and obstacle.** A versioned source span plus a playback-confirmed word boundary will reduce skipped words compared with persisting the generation cursor. A short, adjustable replay may additionally reduce repair when the user was already attending to the question. The obstacles are system timing, alignment and UX. A paragraph bookmark may suffice for some readers. Small repeats can cost more than they help.

**Run it.** In the [interruption simulator](#lab/interruption), compare the generated and heard extents. As a hand-check, give four consecutive words start times of 0, 400, 800 and 1,200 ms. Interrupt after 750 ms of presented audio with generation at 1,500 ms. Flooring each cursor to a word start chooses 400 and 1,200 respectively. The latter jumps 450 ms beyond presentation; the former repeats 350 ms. Test a delayed completion mark arriving after a clear. Track skipped words, repeat duration, answer attribution and manual rewinds. A synthetic receipt proves position arithmetic. To test comprehension, randomize replay policy with consenting listeners and ask where they wanted to resume. **Falsifier:** reject the added replay policy for the target group if a sufficiently precise paired comparison rules out the chosen reduction in repair while replay increases annoyance. Keep the simpler bookmark as a baseline.

## 2 · Which overlapping voice should take the floor?

**Who and why.** A listener saying “mm” may want narration to continue. A listener beginning “wait” may want it to stop. In a shared room another person may be speaking to someone else. An energy detector can see activity in all three. Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 constructs interruption, backchannel, background speech and talking-to-others scenarios. IndicFDB extends full-duplex evaluation across Indian languages with conversational events and synthetic interruption examples, using translated responses in part of its quality evaluation. These are concrete attempts at measuring the problem. Their event definitions and input mixtures delimit what a result tells us about a real room. [Overlap benchmark](#source/overlap) · [IndicFDB](#source/sy-indicfdb)

**Hypothesis and obstacle.** Short-range prosody and conversational context will improve decisions over an energy-only policy at a fixed response time. Voice Activity Projection supplies an approach to predicting future activity, but knowing who next made sound is a different target from knowing whom an utterance addressed. The obstacles are signal separation, intent labeling and evaluation across language communities. A continuously available pause button is a strong baseline in a noisy room. [Voice Activity Projection](#source/vap)

**Run it.** In the [endpoint lab](#lab/endpoint), note which changes an energy threshold can detect. Give two test events the same onset and 300 ms duration. Label one a backchannel with desired action *continue* and the other an interruption with action *yield*. A detector using only that binary activity timeline must take the same action in both cases, so on this balanced pair it misses at least one. Then design a held-out set with consented two-channel examples, native-speaker intent labels, disagreement retained and household-disjoint splits. Compare a timer, context-aware policy and manual override. Record unnecessary stops, missed requests and p95 stop delay. **Falsifier:** if the proposed feature fails to reduce either error at matched delay, with intervals excluding a predeclared worthwhile effect, drop it for that setting. The two-event probe exposes insufficient information. It gives no estimate of how often that ambiguity occurs.

## 3 · Can someone repair an atypical-speech error without repeating a whole turn?

**Who and why.** People whose speech differs from a recognizer’s training distribution may need a different repair path. In a study of 27 participants with speech disorders, Tobin and colleagues found a gap between read and conversational recognition, including with personalized models. Training with conversational material improved recognition in their setup. Earlier work also found that small quantities of speaker-specific material could help some participants, with outcomes varying by speaker and task. These studies support testing both speech modes. They measure recognition accuracy; they leave the effort of correcting an erroneous action as a separate question. [Conversational ASR study](#source/rs-atypical) · [Personalization study](#source/rs-personal)

**Hypothesis and obstacle.** For some users, preserving a typed or tap-to-select repair path after a partial error reduces effort more than additional enrollment. The obstacles include data rights, individual variation, enrollment time, motor accessibility and labeling what a successful interaction means. Personalization can already work well. Touch or text may be inaccessible for a particular person. Allow them to select their preferred alternative.

**Run it.** In the [fixture harness](#lab/evaluation), take one misrecognized utterance. Build two scripted traces with the same error: repeat the complete question, or correct the one disputed entity. Count correction steps, elapsed time, whether the corrected action succeeds and whether the reader keeps their place. Then swap the cost of each input mode to see when the preference reverses. This is a bookkeeping exercise on synthetic error traces. An empirical test needs consented participants, within-person comparisons with their usual interaction method, and their own effort assessments. **Falsifier:** reject a default repair path if confidence bounds rule out the preselected effort benefit, or if it improves the median by making the path unusable for another participant. Report individual outcomes alongside aggregates.

## 4 · Does dramatization help readers follow dialogue?

**Who and why.** A book listener may enjoy a new voice while misattributing dialogue after an interruption. Govender and King tested a secondary-task measure of listening effort, and found that a natural-speech baseline complicated its interpretation. Blizzard Challenge resources include audiobook material and listening-test tools. Neither gives a direct estimate of comprehension for interruptible, multi-character reading. Existing tools can still supply strong baselines: a clear single voice, explicit speaker cues and accessible replay. [Dual-task study](#source/rs-effort) · [Blizzard materials](#source/rs-blizzard)

**Hypothesis and obstacle.** Stable character direction, such as consistent pace and emphasis, improves speaker attribution at the same text and playback level. Casting more voices alone could make identity less stable. The obstacles are long-form control, directing effort, listener preference and evaluation cost.

**Run it.** Write two original exchanges of similar length and turn structure. For example: *Rae closed the window. “I heard the train,” she said. Dev checked the clock. “The last one left an hour ago.”* Prepare a single-voice and a directed version of each with permission to use the recording. Keep the text and transcript available, match level and timing where possible, and vary which version of each passage a person hears first. Each person hears only one rendering per passage to limit practice effects. Interrupt once, then ask who mentioned time and where narration should resume. Measure attribution, comprehension, replay count and preference separately. **Falsifier:** if a powered comparison excludes a meaningful attribution gain at matched comprehension and shows higher production effort, direct that effort elsewhere. A one-listener pilot checks the protocol and catches ambiguous questions.

## Check your reasoning

**An experiment’s result is noisy and includes zero. Has it disproved a useful effect?** A wide interval may include both zero and a practically important benefit. Increase independent participants or refine the measure. Set the smallest effect worth pursuing before observing the results.

**Which of these questions can a deterministic fixture settle?** It can settle a cursor calculation or whether a policy with identical inputs can yield different actions. It can expose a missing observable. Claims about comfort, intention or comprehension require evidence from the people who experience them.
