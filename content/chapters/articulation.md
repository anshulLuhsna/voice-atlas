Hold a steady vowel, then change the pitch while trying to keep the vowel recognizable. Next keep roughly the same pitch while moving from “ee” to “ah.” You have changed two different things. Expelled air can set the vocal folds into roughly periodic motion. The repeating excitation has a fundamental frequency, F0. If successive cycles are 8 milliseconds apart, F0 is approximately `1 / 0.008 s = 125 Hz`. Opening the jaw or moving the tongue changes the shape of the air passage above the folds. That tube reinforces some frequencies more than others. Its resonances, visible as broad bands of energy in voiced speech, are **formants**.

## A source and a moving filter

As a first approximation, the radiated spectrum is the spectrum of the excitation multiplied by the response of the vocal tract and radiation at the lips. In a voiced vowel, harmonics might lie at 125, 250, 375 Hz and so on. A tract resonance near 700 Hz can boost harmonics in its neighborhood. If F0 rises to 150 Hz the harmonic spacing changes while a roughly fixed tract shape retains a similar envelope. A whisper supplies noisy excitation and can still carry a recognizable vowel. The approximation becomes less faithful during rapid transitions, turbulent flow and coupling between the source and tract.

Consonants involve particular constrictions. A stop closes the tract, builds pressure and releases it in a burst. A fricative narrows the passage enough to create sustained turbulence. For a nasal, air can escape through the nose. **Place** describes where the constriction occurs, **manner** describes how the air is restricted and **voicing** describes vocal-fold activity. Aspiration is audible airflow after a release. A useful acoustic measurement is the delay between release and the start of regular voicing in an appropriate stop context. Position and speaking rate affect that delay.

## A difference a language uses

A phone is a realized speech sound. A phoneme is a contrastive category in a particular language. A minimal pair supplies a test: change a single sound in the same surrounding sequence and see whether the change can distinguish words. The UCLA Hindi archive gives सात, “seven,” and साथ, “along,” with a dental stop and its aspirated counterpart. Entries 23–24 illustrate dental त in आता and retroflex ट in आटा. The tip or blade makes a constriction near the teeth for the dental sound and farther back for the retroflex sound. A transcription convention that maps both to an English-style *t* loses information a Hindi listener may use. These examples come from an elicited list with one recorded setting. [Hindi examples, entries 3–6 and 23–24](#source/sp-hi-words) · [Phonemic contrast](#source/phonemes)

Speech rarely arrives as discrete blocks. In *key* the upcoming front vowel influences where the speaker closes the tongue for the preceding consonant. This **coarticulation** spreads cues across the acoustic boundary an annotator might draw. Listeners use neighboring sounds, context and familiarity with a speaker to interpret such cues. A fixed threshold on F0 or a formant across all speakers will consequently miss distinctions. An accent includes systematic pronunciation differences. A dialect can additionally differ in grammar and vocabulary. A reference transcript that “corrects” a dialect form silently changes what a recognition test measures.

## Meaning across a phrase

Read “I said the **blue** folder” and “**I** said the blue folder.” The likely correction shifts from color to speaker. Stress and phrase-level prominence can recruit duration, intensity and pitch together. Rhythm emerges from their timing, reductions and pauses. Intonation operates over a phrase, while in a tone language pitch can also distinguish words. A rising contour alone is an unreliable universal question label. The listener's interpretation depends on the language, position and conversational setting. [Tone and intonation](#source/sp-prosody)

For a bilingual listener, switching languages within one clause can be routine. A decoder forced to choose one language for an entire turn can erase the switch or transliterate a name inconsistently. MUCS supplies Hindi–English and Bengali–English code-switched material. IndicVoices separates elicitation styles including read, extempore and conversational speech. A test on read monolingual sentences generalizes only as far as its sampling allows. [MUCS challenge](#source/sp-mucs) · [IndicVoices collection](#source/indic)

## Exercise · annotate two parallel channels

Write a three-second example with speaker A saying “the blue … uh, green one” while B says “mm” over the pause. Use two time-aligned rows. Label the repair, the hesitation, B's overlap and any audible breath or laugh separately. Then produce one transcript for verbatim study and another for searching final intent. Expect the verbatim version to retain *blue* and the repair. Expect an intent index to prefer *green*. Collapsing both into one cleaned transcript removes the evidence that makes the correction intelligible. In a reader, that evidence can decide whether to interrupt narration or continue.

## Check your reasoning

**A speaker doubles F0. Must the vowel change?** Doubling changes harmonic spacing. The tract shape can stay approximately fixed, so the spectral envelope can still support the vowel identity. With high F0 fewer harmonics sample that envelope, which can complicate estimation.

**Two annotators disagree on the start time of a phone. Does one necessarily have the wrong word?** Transitions can carry cues for both neighbors. Give the annotation a tolerance or uncertain interval, and score the lexical decision separately from boundary precision.
