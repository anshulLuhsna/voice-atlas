Pick a result you would change a design decision for. “The agent is better at turn-taking” is too broad. “Given the same recordings and decision latency, the candidate cuts off fewer unfinished turns than a silence timer” tells you what to build, what to hold constant and what would change your mind. A professional profile tells you where to begin. Methods, data and code establish what the result means.

## Follow a result across four research programs

**Gabriel Skantze, KTH.** Conversation gives you two time-varying signals, one for each person. In the Voice Activity Projection work with Erik Ekstedt, the training target is derived from future voice activity; labeling each moment with a human judgment of “done” would be expensive and ambiguous. Draw the two channels and their future windows. Decide what counts as a speaker holding the floor, what counts as a shift and whether a short listener response changes either label. The activity target can be learned from recordings, while the desired intervention still needs a policy and an interaction study. A useful first ablation compares acoustic cues with a silence baseline on the same turns. [Professional profile](#source/skantze) · [Voice Activity Projection](#source/vap)

**Hema A. Murthy, IIT Madras.** Prakash and Murthy trained a generic synthesizer on multiple languages within a family and adapted it to another language. Their experiment asks how linguistic relatedness and shared speech data affect adaptation. Inspect the target language, held-out material, duration available for adaptation and listening protocol. To challenge the proposed transfer mechanism, compare source language sets at the same total hours and speaker count. Include unfamiliar names and contrasts that native listeners judge, and separate speaker similarity from pronunciation. The paper’s data budget is a result for those languages and conditions; another language, writing system or speaker can change it. [Professional profile](#source/murthy) · [Indic synthesis study](#source/rs-prakash)

**Shinji Watanabe, Carnegie Mellon.** ESPnet makes a research claim inspectable by packaging the steps from corpus preparation through scoring. A checkpoint alone cannot tell you how text was normalized, which speakers were held out or whether a language model rescored the hypotheses. Start with the original paper, select one recipe in the repository and trace its input list into the final scorer. When comparing implementations, rerun the baseline with the same vocabulary, decoding beam and score normalization. If only the scorer changed, attribute the delta to evaluation. Record the repository revision because recipes evolve. [Professional profile](#source/watanabe) · [ESPnet paper](#source/rs-espnet-paper) · [ESPnet project](#source/espnet)

**Simon King, University of Edinburgh.** Govender and King paired speech listening with a secondary reaction-time task to investigate effort. Listeners also heard natural recordings. That baseline matters: the expected ordering becomes ambiguous when natural speech fails to yield the quickest secondary-task responses. The timing may reflect changes in sustained attention as well as effort. For Murmur, the analogous question is whether character voices help listeners attribute dialogue without increasing fatigue. A preference vote, a speaker-attribution question and a secondary reaction time measure separate phenomena. [Professional profile](#source/rs-king) · [Dual-task study](#source/rs-effort)

## Reduce the first reproduction to one controlled contrast

Imagine evaluating an endpoint policy on six **constructed** mid-sentence pauses of 100, 220, 310, 390, 560 and 720 ms. Each is followed by more words from the same person. A policy that closes the turn once a silence reaches 300 ms cuts off four of the six. At 500 ms it cuts off two. Now add six real turn endings long enough for either threshold to fire. The slower policy adds 200 ms of waiting to each completed turn. A lower cutoff count with a longer wait establishes a tradeoff on the fixture. To claim better prediction at a fixed delay, compare both policies at matched response delay or report their whole tradeoff curve. Use the [endpoint experiment](#lab/endpoint) to see how an energy threshold adds a second source of error.

A **replication** repeats the intended setup on fresh or independently assembled data. A **reproduction** in this chapter means rerunning a documented setup and checking whether its reported result can be recovered. State which you attempted. Prefer speaker- or conversation-level splits, a paired comparison, recorded seeds and a disclosed hardware budget. Match historical dependencies when investigating a historical number. For a large training run you cannot afford, reproduce a metric or ablation from released predictions and clearly delimit the result.

## Trace groups into materials you can inspect

KTH’s Speech, Music and Hearing department connects perception, production and interaction. An experiment in face-to-face conversation may rely on gaze that Murmur cannot observe. Edinburgh’s Centre for Speech Technology Research hosts Blizzard Challenge listening tools and results, including audiobook datasets with edition-specific conditions. Compare the listeners, languages and material before joining results across years. Kyutai’s Moshi work provides a contrasting direct speech model; trace its concurrent input and output streams and identify which part produces system delay. These are three different ways to enter the literature: find a sensing assumption, reuse a listening protocol, or inspect a model’s timing structure. [KTH department](#source/rs-tmh) · [Edinburgh centre](#source/rs-cstr) · [Blizzard resources](#source/rs-blizzard) · [Kyutai](#source/rs-kyutai) · [Moshi paper](#source/moshi)

You can engage by building a reproducible example before anyone sees it. For Praat, give a script, synthetic signal and the analysis window. For pyannote.audio, give exact overlap intervals, scoring collar and pipeline version. For SpeechBrain, follow the project’s guidelines and include a runnable small test or documentation example. Specify model access and data terms separately from the code license. Keep private conversations out of a public issue or example. ## Exercise · write a one-page reproduction record

Choose one of the four papers above. Write its claim in one sentence, naming the comparison and population. List inputs you can obtain, anything that needs permission, the baseline, scoring unit and stop condition. Use one tiny example to confirm the metric first. In the [evaluation harness](#lab/evaluation), try scoring a single transcript edit and predict its effect on the task. Expected outcome: a record another engineer can challenge, including one observation that would contradict the claim. If the materials only support rescoring, record that boundary and state exactly which additional artifact would support the next step.

## Check your reasoning

**A later release beats the paper’s original score. What does that establish?** It establishes a difference between two reported measurements. To assign credit to a model change, match the test split, normalizer, decoding recipe and scoring protocol and repeat the comparison.

**A result fails on a new accent. Does that invalidate the original study?** It changes the claim you can make about transfer. Check whether the original claim covered that population. Show the new slice with counts, uncertainty and a paired baseline, and investigate whether data or measurement explains the difference.
