# Scope and limitations

The 24 lessons form a complete authored first edition within their stated learning goals. They cover all ten requested subject groups and the eight dedicated deep dives. The material has automated checks and a source audit, but it has no independent expert peer review.

The default browser labs use deterministic signals, calculations and simulations. They test inspectable mechanics. Human listening, live recognition, microphone capture, deployed telephony and GPU serving need separate environments and evaluation.

The default acoustic adaptation recipe trains a real small classifier on synthetic categories. Its measured results apply to its generator and test groups. The optional Whisper recipe validates local data, but its pretrained training path remains unverified. Model weights, permitted speech datasets and optional dependencies are supplied by the learner.

Source records carry review dates. Product prices and entitlements can change by region, plan and time. The reader comparison uses public vendor claims and proposed listening protocols. A comparative human study remains an exercise. Cost calculations with assumed inputs are labeled estimates.

The source index contains 121 annotated entries. Six URLs have separate entries because different lessons use different annotations. A final audit revisited a sample of substantive claims. It did not independently reproduce every cited study or recheck every external URL in one sweep.

Reading progress uses browser LocalStorage at one origin. It supports completion state, the current reading location and reset. Cross-device synchronization and progress export are future extensions. A changed edition can move text relative to a saved position.

Automated accessibility checks found no violations on the tested routes after fixes. Axe could not resolve contrast for some SVG text. Manual inspection and a calculated text/background contrast check supplement those cases. Full screen-reader testing and a broader browser/device matrix remain future work.

Possible advanced extensions include microphone-array beamforming, permutation-invariant speech separation, multilingual pronunciation studies, a measured local inference profile and a consented real-model adaptation run. The manifest records these as planned extensions without adding empty lesson routes.
